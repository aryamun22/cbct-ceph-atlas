Corrected diagnosis

    Already at the strong end

    The parts of the skeleton that need nothing

    The outer frame and the back half of the face — ramus, angle, jaw length, cheekbone width, chin, symmetry — are the hard-to-build parts, and they are already at or beyond the male norm. What is off is the front-middle: a maxilla that is narrow and a few millimetres back, a short upper lip and midface, and the bite. Three of these strengths (flat jaw plane, high posterior height ratio, forward chin) are one horizontal growth pattern; they read as strength once the front of the face has the height and projection to match them.

    The "15 mm behind" question

    Andrews' GALL: where the maxilla and incisors sit relative to the forehead

    Rule (Andrews, Six Elements, Element II; sourced via Wu et al. 2023 and the Andrews 2008/2015 papers). The Goal Anterior Limit Line is a true vertical in natural head position. It passes through the forehead's FFA point (the midpoint of the clinical forehead) when the forehead inclination is ≤ 7°; for every degree beyond 7° it moves 0.6 mm forward, but never beyond glabella. The FA point of the maxillary central incisor should sit on it.

    Applied here. Forehead inclination ° (scanner vertical) / ° (Frankfort frame). The adjustment ( mm) exceeds the FFA-to-glabella gap ( mm), so the cap applies: the GALL is the glabellar vertical, mm anterior of nasion. The cap is reached at any inclination above 18°, so the GALL does not depend on the forehead being clipped by the field of view above mm (a trichion-based inclination of 25–29° would move it by at most ~1 mm).

    Simulated movement plan

    Maxilla wider, forward and longer; mandible left where it is

    Built in a rigid-body surgical simulator on the landmarks above, after three competing plans (Andrews + airway, Arnett soft-tissue harmony, maximum vertical) were scored by an aesthetics judge and a stability/airway judge and merged; about 420 simulator runs, independently re-derived to 0.02 mm. Constraints: the pharyngeal airway is small (70 mm² minimum cross-section), so the mandible may not move back more than 2 mm; natural head position was not recorded, so position-against-the-forehead numbers are given at 0°, 3.9° and 8° chin-down.

    What it produces

    Considered and rejected

    • Mandibular setback. To close the bite alone it takes B 5.7 mm and pogonion 5.1 mm back — over the airway limit — and leaves the midface flat and the face no longer.
    • Chin advancement. The chin is already the most forward point of the face.
    • Le Fort II / III. Over-projects the upper midface (orbital rim to +1.6–2.7 SD) with a 10 mm nasofrontal step; the rims are not truly deficient — a 3 mm onlay covers them.
    • High (quadrangular) Le Fort I. Subpupil area overshoots; reported vertical relapse 62–158 %.
    • Rotation-only plans (downgraft without advance). Chin 4–7 mm back, bite still Class III, airway worse.
    • Inverted-L ramus lengthening. Flattens an already flat jaw plane on an already long ramus.
    • Malar implants. Cheekbone projection is already at the norm.

    Before anything is fixed

    • Natural head position — one profile photograph with a plumb line. The advance is right for ~4° chin-down; at a level Frankfort posture it should drop to 4.5–5 mm.
    • A scan after expansion — whatever the expander actually drops and drifts the maxilla changes the Le Fort I by several millimetres; totals stay the same.
    • A sleep study — moderate or severe sleep apnoea would favour a larger bimaxillary advance at the cost of the chin and incisor targets.

    Sequence: expansion (≈1 month active, 6 months retention) → orthodontics 9–14 months → one operation → orthodontics 6–9 months → stage-2 implants and nose 6–12 months later. About 2–2.5 years.

    Closing the facial-height deficit

    Face height to the male mean; the ratio still reads broad

    A second round extended the simulator (lower-border osteotomies, vertical implants, lip and incisor-intrusion controls, parallel downgrade with the mandible following) and ran three height strategies through the same judges. The recommended stack, on top of the plan above: vertical genioplasty 7.0 mm (was 4.5), anterior downgraft 4.0 mm grafted (was 2.5) with 0.8 mm upper-incisor intrusion and 1.8 mm lower-incisor extrusion, posterior maxilla +0.5 mm instead of 1.0 mm impaction, bite seated at 2.5 mm overjet. No new implants, no ramus surgery.

    Why it stops there

    • The missing height is above the jaws. Midface (soft nasion to subnasale) and upper-lip length are the short segments; no jaw operation lengthens either, and the lip gains about 1 mm.
    • Lip seal is the binding limit. Each extra millimetre of downgraft parts the lips about 0.6 mm; the stack sits at a 3.3 mm gap, borderline at the worst soft-tissue bound, settling to 2.4.
    • Tooth show is capped at about 4 mm; more downgraft needs more intrusion than orthodontics delivers.
    • The chin is at its norm at 7 mm; more makes it long.
    • The ramus cannot follow a larger parallel downgraft without a 4 mm step or a stretched sling.
    • About a quarter of the gain is the lips parting rather than tissue, and every millimetre lands below the nose, so the upper-to-lower third ratio moves from 0.89 to 0.80.

    The index against several male norms

    Part of the gap is definition: measured from bony-nasion level the same face reads 88.7 at surgery. The rest is soft cheekbone width (144.8 mm), not height. Leanness (≈3 mm less soft width) would add ≈1.7 index points; zygomatic reduction was rejected because it narrows the cheekbones the plan keeps.

    Maxillary expansion

    What 10 mm of expansion does on its own — and whether it can replace the Le Fort I

    Screw opening is not bone. Meta-analyses of bone-borne expanders in late adolescents and adults: the suture splits in ≈93 % of cases; the gain is ≈49 % basal bone, ≈8 % alveolar and ≈43 % tooth tipping, with a typical basal gain of 2.3 mm (1.7–3.0). Surgically assisted expansion in adults gives ≈7.5 mm at the molars with ≈3.5 mm skeletal and ≈30 % dental relapse. Ten millimetres of activation therefore puts roughly five into the basal bone; closing the full transverse deficit may need over-expansion and a second step.

    What happens to the rest of the face depends on how far the maxilla drops and drifts while it widens. With the mandible left to find its own bite, every millimetre of drop props the jaw open on the back teeth, swinging the chin down and back. That looks like progress — a longer face, a smaller Class III — but the front teeth stop meeting and the tongue base moves back. The airway limit here is 2 mm.

    Can expansion, with or without a facemask, remove the need for a Le Fort I?

    Mostly no. A typical response leaves the bite skeletally Class III with the front teeth apart. A strong response can close the bite on paper, but most of the correction comes from the mandible swinging 3.7–5 mm back — the move the airway rules out — with the midface forward 2–5 mm instead of 8 and 5+ mm of tooth showing once the incisors are uprighted. A large expander response shrinks the Le Fort I (to ≈4 mm advance with posterior impaction if it gives 4 mm down / 3 mm forward) rather than deleting it. Decide on the post-expansion scan and the sleep study.

    Method and corrections

    How this version was produced, and what changed

    Pipeline

    • Volume: 668×668×445 voxels, 0.45 mm isotropic, HU-calibrated. Bone threshold 400 HU, skin −450 HU.
    • Landmarking: for each of four regions, two agents located every landmark independently — one by reading thin slices in three planes, one algorithmically from the volume — without access to any earlier result.
    • Verification: one adversarial agent per landmark re-rendered both candidates in three planes, re-derived the point from its definition, and chose or corrected it; each verifier's note records the disagreement between the two candidates and what structure the final point sits on.
    • Frame: Frankfort plane by least squares through both porions and orbitale (residuals ≤ 0.2 mm); midsagittal plane perpendicular to FH through nasion and sella.
    • Norms: adult-male means and SDs with sources (Steiner, McNamara, Ricketts, Holdaway, Jarabak/Björk, Tweed, Legan–Burstone, Arnett STCA, Farkas, Penn, Andrews). Where only pooled or range values exist, the table says so and gives no z.
    • Checks: two independent agents re-derived the frame, every coordinate and every headline number from the landmark voxels. Round 1 found eleven issues (occlusal-plane contact point, an alar landmark definition mismatch, unverifiable SDs for U1-NA/L1-NB and the Jarabak ratio, a missing Ricketts row, the Holdaway chin-thickness definition, the U1-to-A-vertical measuring point, the provisional facial axis, the facial-index classification, a yaw sign and an overbite sign in notes); all were corrected in round 2. A third pass checked this page against the analysis file.

    Corrections versus the first version of this study

    • Arnett midface norms: the first version compared the orbital rim, cheekbone and subpupil points with the female norms (−18.7, −20.6, −14.8). The male norms are −22.4, −25.2, −18.4. The "5–9 mm infraorbital deficit" shrinks to 2–3 mm at the rim (about 1 SD) and ~4 mm under the pupil (4.6 R / 3.9 L); the cheekbone point reads normal but sits 5 mm from the subpupil point on the same slope, so the honest summary is one infraorbital reading 1–2 SD behind.
    • Pogonion was 2.8 mm too posterior; nasion is now on the frontonasal suture, 4.5 mm above the concavity point used before; zygion was on temporal bone (bony Zy–Zy 132.1, not 141); overjet/overbite are 0.6 / −0.6 (edge-to-edge), not 1.5 / 3.5; chin pad is 14–15 mm (normal), not 17; nasolabial angle ≈ 65° (range 59–73 by construction), not 97°.
    • Ramus: Ar–Go 58 mm is +2.8 SD on Björk's norm — long, not "normal". Gonial angle Co-Go-Me 121°, not 117°; Ar-Go-Me 128° (norm 130 ± 6, normal).
    • Diagnosis moves from "mandible normal, maxilla neutral" to an 8–12 mm Class III jaw discrepancy: mildly retrusive maxilla (SNA 79.4, A −1.6 to N-perp, −10.9 to the GALL where a balanced male sits at −7 to −10), a mandible of normal length (Co–Gn 130 mm ×1.08 vs 134 ± 7) rotated forward by a horizontal growth pattern (FMA 14.5°, Björk sum 383°, S-Go:N-Me 73 %) so that the chin reads +11.8 mm to N-perp in the Frankfort frame, ≈ +4.8 mm at the scanned pitch, and +2.5 mm to the GALL where a balanced male sits at ≈ −9; brachyfacial; maxillary transverse deficiency; narrow airway. Whether the chin is called "prognathic" depends on the reference; on every reference with a norm it is 4–12 mm ahead, and the jaw discrepancy does not change.
    • Remaining low-confidence items: columella point (conf 0.5, ±2 mm along the columella, so the nasolabial angle is approximate), Pt point (facial axis provisional), antegonial notches (absent), left alar base (alare landmarked, Arnett norm not applied), thin bone (ANS, sella floor) not fully rendered in the 3D model.

    What a completeness critic flagged, and what is still missing

    • Framing. "Retrusive maxilla" rests on SNA (which is depressed ~2° by the steep 8.8° SN–FH); the Frankfort- and Arnett-based maxillary rows are all within 1 SD. The maxillary deficit is mild; the chin's forward position is the larger component in the Frankfort frame and roughly equal in a chin-down NHP.
    • Landmark dependence. Articular angle (z −4), Björk sum and Ar–Go depend on articulare (conf 0.6, left–right 4.7 mm apart); U1-SN/U1-FH and the whole incisor-uprighting estimate depend on U1 apex (5.7 mm disagreement); the nasolabial angle on the columella point (conf 0.5); the facial axis on an approximated Pt (conf 0.4); Me′ drives the soft-tissue heights and the facial index. The headline pattern (brachyfacial, Class III, proclined uppers) survives all of them; the exact z-scores do not.
    • Norm provenance. Jarabak's linear norms are Björk's adolescent values, Arnett's heights carry cephalometric magnification, several Holdaway/Legan–Burstone "male" sets are secondary sources, and the subject's ancestry is not recorded. Face-height verdicts therefore range from "normal" (Jarabak) to "short" (Farkas, McNamara).
    • Occlusal plane. The reverse-sloping occlusal plane (−5°) is mostly the 3.9° FH pitch; Wits varies −5.3 to −9.8 with the plane chosen. Class III on all variants.
    • Cheekbone′ / subpupil′ are two skin points 5 mm apart on one slope; treat them as one infraorbital reading (~1–2 SD behind), not as "cheek normal, subpupil retruded".
    • Not run: COGS hard-tissue analysis; Downs with norms; Ricketts Xi/Pm/corpus axis; molar and canine Class III in mm; dental midlines vs facial midline (both ~1–1.5 mm left); curve of Spee; lower-incisor decompensation in the advancement arithmetic (would add 2–3 mm of reverse overjet); clockwise-rotation modelling of the maxillomandibular complex (the natural plan for this pattern: retracts Pog and advances A at once); soft-tissue response to the advancement scenarios; genioplasty sizing; pharyngeal airway from this CBCT (70 mm² minimum area on the December 2025 scan is the working figure).
    • Given, not re-measured here: the Penn transverse widths (59.0 / 64.5 mm) that carry the 10.5 mm expansion figure — from an earlier manual pass on the same volume.
    • Data requests: a lateral photograph in natural head position with a plumb line (fixes the GALL numbers); a clinical decision on whether the 1.6° ear–orbit roll is posture or a real cant; dental models or an intraoral scan; the sleep study.
    Scope. Geometry only. This is not a radiological report and the scan was not read for pathology. Norms are population means; treatment decisions belong to the surgeon and orthodontist with models, photographs, a sleep study and the patient in the room.
